First rung checked: our target is Erdős #7

Earlier tonight we announced a formal verification lab and said we would name our first target when we chose it. The choice is made, and the first rung is already checked.

Back to the home page · More articles

Our target is Erdős Problem #7, the Erdős Selfridge odd covering problem: does there exist a covering system of the integers whose moduli are all odd, all distinct, and all greater than 1? Erdős and Selfridge asked it decades ago. Nobody knows the answer in either direction, and the problem still carries the $25 prize Erdős attached to it.

The honest way to enter a field is to reproduce what the field already knows, on your own machines, under your own gate. In July 2026, Ibrahim Mian and Shayaan Siddique published a kernel checked exclusion (arXiv:2607.25628): any odd covering system must have an lcm exceeding 10,000, because every one of the 23 odd abundant candidates at or below that bound carries a finite certificate that refutes it. Their code is public.

Tonight our lab built their development from source and ran the gate we run on everything. The full build completed, and the axiom audit passed: all 63 published theorems close over exactly the three standard axioms of Lean, with no gaps and no shortcuts, including the theorem that carries the result to the problem's official statement in the Formal Conjectures collection. The machinery now also runs on our own current toolchain, where the extension work will live.

What this is, and what it is not. It is a reproduction: independent verification, in Charleston, Illinois, of the strongest published result on this problem. It is not a solution, and this lab will never announce one it cannot compile. The problem remains open in both directions.

The road ahead is already surveyed. The published certificate method provably stalls at three numbers just above the frontier: 10395, 12285, and 17325. Passing them requires sharper certificates, new mathematics rather than more computing. That is the work now, alongside a search in the other direction, in case a covering system is out there waiting to be found instead of excluded.

The model proposes. The proof checker decides. First rung checked.
